Where the sources disagree, and what we used

  • How much of Wyoming's herd died in the winter of 1886-87 is disputed: T. A. Larson's estimate is about 15 per cent statewide (WyoHistory), while a National Park Service page says the average Wyoming operation lost 75 per cent. Use Larson's 15 per cent as the statewide figure and the named ranch losses (Swan 25 per cent or more, Mason-Lovell half, Roosevelt over half) as specifics; never say 'most cattle in Wyoming died'.
  • Brisbin's five-year profit: his own table shows a net profit of $36,500 on $25,000 (146 per cent); WyoHistory summarises his promise as 156 per cent over five years. Quote the table figure.
  • The Texas price: McCoy says five or six dollars in Texas (and one to two dollars at the worst); Kansapedia says $2; Slatta says $4. The title's '$5 cow' follows McCoy; the Chicago end ($40 to $50) follows Kansapedia and Slatta, and McCoy's own 1867 Chicago figure was $24 to $28 a head.
  • Montana's losses (a widely repeated 362,000 head, more than half the herd) come from popular sources only and are not in this sheet.
  • The Swan dividend and price-per-head series come from a single WyoHistory article; treat them as medium confidence until checked against the company's reports (University of Wyoming American Heritage Center).
  • Brisbin's OCR text prints the dollar sign as a section mark (§); the excerpts keep it.

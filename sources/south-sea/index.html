Where the sources disagree, and what we used

  • The peak: The National Archives says about £1,050 after £950 at the end of June; the New York Fed says £1,050 in June; Britannica says more than 1,000 in August; History of Parliament says about 1,000 in July. Say 'about £1,000 in the summer of 1720' or 'about £1,050 at the peak' without a month.
  • The low after the crash: £190 (National Archives), £175 by September (New York Fed), 150 by October (History of Parliament), 124 by December (Britannica). Each is a different date; quote one with its date, and use 124 in December for 'back where it started'.
  • Dates: England used the old Julian calendar in 1720, 11 days behind the Continent. Frehen et al. give the crash as 19 September new style. Prefer month-level dates ('in September') unless the source is English and old-style.
  • Newton's loss: Odlyzko (2019) estimates about £12,000 to £14,000, at least £10,000, and says the folk tale is almost certainly right; Helen Paul (Gresham, 2021) calls it a myth that Newton lost a lot, 'according to Newton scholars'. The '£20,000' figure is family lore from his niece, printed 1797. Say 'Newton lost heavily; one historian's estimate is more than £10,000' and never state £20,000 as fact.
  • The famous 'I can calculate the motions of the heavenly bodies, but not the madness of people' is not what the oldest source says; use 'he could not calculate the madness of the people' and say it is reported second-hand.
  • The number of 'bubble' companies floated in 1720 (often given as 190 or so) is in popular sources only; Hoppit's own figure is hedged ('perhaps') and did not survive the text extraction. Not in this sheet; do not give a number.
  • Who was ruined: Hoppit argues losses fell mainly on the wealthy and the wider economy was not badly hurt; Stukeley wrote of thousands of families ruined. Present both.
  • Committee of Secrecy excerpts are from 1721 print via OCR; the long s and broken characters were normalised, and 'l.' is the printed pound sign. Sums are pounds of stock (nominal), not cash.
  • Debt figures: £9.471 million (Munro) and 'around £9,500,000' (National Archives) for 1711 agree; Munro's £31.58 million is the debt the 1720 scheme aimed to convert, not the amount actually converted.
  • The conversion uses a price index that before 1900 is a reconstructed composite (Bank of England millennium data); round it ('about £200 today for every pound of 1720') and say it is a price comparison, not a wage or GDP comparison.
  • The company's slave trading is part of the story and must be stated plainly, not as colour; the St Mark figures are a single voyage, not the company's total, which is not in this sheet.
